Abstract—In modern talent acquisition workflows, automated Applicant Tracking Systems are universally deployed to screen massive volumes of candidate submissions. However, traditional screening engines depend heavily on exact lexical keyword matching and static boolean rules. These conventional techniques exhibit critical failure modes, including severe vocabulary mismatch, complete insensitivity to contextual semantic nuance, vulnerability to artificial keyword stuffing, and an utter absence of constructive diagnostic feedback for job applicants. This paper presents ResumeMind, an end-to-end deep learning and natural language processing system engineered to perform automated resume-to-job matching, granular technical competency extraction, and automated career recommendation. The proposed architecture employs a bidirectional transformer encoder to derive dense token-level representations across professional curricula vitae and unstructured job postings. To transform variable-length token representations into fixed-dimensional document embeddings without distortion from padding artifacts, an attention-weighted mean pooling formulation is derived and implemented. A dual-objective scoring framework combines deep semantic similarity in seventy-six-dimensional latent space with compiled word-boundary regular expression matching across an established technical taxonomy. Empirical benchmarks across technical recruitment domains indicate that the proposed system achieves superior semantic alignment compared to legacy keyword baselines, remains robust under synonym substitution, and generates transparent skill gap diagnostics that enhance algorithmic fairness and candidate career development.
Keywords—Deep Learning, Natural Language Processing, BERT Embeddings, Resume Matching, Applicant Tracking Systems

I. Introduction

The contemporary recruitment landscape is characterized by an unprecedented asymmetry between application volume and human evaluation capacity. Corporate job requisitions frequently attract hundreds to thousands of submissions within hours of publication. To cope with this cognitive bottleneck, enterprises depend almost exclusively on automated Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) as the initial screening gatekeeper.

Notwithstanding their pervasive deployment, conventional ATS architectures rely on rudimentary information retrieval heuristics designed decades ago. Primarily rooted in literal string equality, basic regular expression parsers, and term frequency-inverse document frequency (TF-IDF) scoring, these legacy systems prioritize superficial token repetition over substantive professional competency. Consequently, a highly qualified candidate who articulates their background using alternative industry nomenclature (such as describing deep neural architectures rather than explicitly repeating the token deep learning) is routinely rejected by automated filters. This creates an unacceptably high rate of false negatives for recruiting organizations while fostering deep disenfranchisement among job seekers.

Furthermore, traditional recruitment pipelines function as uninterpretable black boxes. Candidates whose profiles are filtered out receive generic rejection communiques devoid of actionable diagnostic feedback regarding missing technical competencies or prerequisite qualifications.

To address these systemic shortcomings, this research introduces ResumeMind, a novel deep learning framework that reformulates resume screening as a dual-objective optimization task encompassing dense semantic alignment and explicit domain entity validation.

II. Literature Review

A. Evolution of Applicant Tracking Systems

Early candidate screening platforms developed in the late 1990s and early 2000s functioned purely as inverted-index database search engines. Recruiters executed Boolean queries consisting of mandatory keywords combined with logical operators. Salton and Buckley established foundational term-weighting approaches in vector space models, demonstrating that inverse document frequency could mitigate common-word distortion. However, statistical bag-of-words formulations inherently discard syntactic structure, word order, and semantic relations.

During the subsequent decade, researchers explored distributional semantic models, notably Word2Vec by Mikolov et al. and GloVe by Pennington et al. Le and Mikolov extended continuous vector representations to arbitrary paragraph lengths via Doc2Vec. While Doc2Vec marked an improvement over sparse lexical counts, it relied on shallow neural architectures that could not model complex bidirectional dependencies or resolve contextual polysemy.

B. Contextual Transformers and Sentence Embeddings

A transformative paradigm shift occurred with the introduction of the self-attention mechanism by Vaswani et al. and the subsequent development of BERT by Devlin et al. Reimers and Gurevych solved the computational complexity bottleneck of pairwise document similarity by proposing Sentence-BERT (SBERT), computing semantically meaningful sentence embeddings comparable via cosine distance in sub-millisecond latency.

III. Methodology

A. Document Ingestion and Preprocessing

Candidate resumes are submitted either as structured plain text or Portable Document Format (PDF) files. The ingestion module incorporates pdfplumber to extract layout-aware character streams while preserving paragraph structure. The extracted raw text is sanitized through Unicode normalization, stripping non-printable ASCII artifacts.

B. Contextual Transformer Vectorization

To generate dense semantic document vectors, the system employs the pretrained sentence-transformers/bert-base-nli-mean-tokens model. Given input token sequence $T = [t_1, t_2, \dots, t_N]$ where $N \le 512$, the transformer encoder outputs hidden states:

$\mathbf{H} = \text{Encoder}(T) = [\mathbf{h}_1, \mathbf{h}_2, \dots, \mathbf{h}_N] \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 768}$
(1)

C. Attention-Weighted Mean Pooling

To aggregate token states into a single document vector without skewing from padding, attention-weighted mean pooling performs element-wise multiplication by the binary attention mask $m_i$:

$\mathbf{u} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{h}_i \cdot m_i}{\max\left(\epsilon, \sum_{i=1}^N m_i\right)}$
(2)

D. Angular Cosine Semantic Proximity

Given the resume embedding $\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{R}^{768}$ and the target job description embedding $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^{768}$, semantic proximity is defined as:

$S_{\text{semantic}} = \left( \frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{u}\|_2 \|\mathbf{v}\|_2} \right) \times 100$
(3)

E. Boundary-Enforced Skill Overlap Formulation

In parallel with transformer embedding, text is evaluated by a compiled regular expression engine over a curated technical taxonomy enforcing word boundaries to prevent substring false matches:

$S_{\text{skill}} = \left( \frac{|\mathcal{R} \cap \mathcal{J}|}{|\mathcal{J}|} \right) \times 100$
(4)
$\mathcal{S}_{\text{matched}} = \mathcal{R} \cap \mathcal{J}, \quad \mathcal{S}_{\text{missing}} = \mathcal{J} \setminus \mathcal{R}$
(5)

F. Composite Scoring and Job Ranking

The composite compatibility score $S_{\text{final}}$ is formulated as a convex linear combination:

$S_{\text{final}} = 0.60 \cdot S_{\text{semantic}} + 0.40 \cdot S_{\text{skill}}$
(6)

IV. System Architecture

The system architecture follows a decoupled three-tier pattern consisting of an interactive Streamlit presentation layer, a Python business logic tier, and a PyTorch / Hugging Face deep learning inference tier. The model weights are preserved in memory using Streamlit's @st.cache_resource decorator, achieving sub-second matching latency on CPU hardware.

V. Results and Discussions

A. Experimental Setup and Benchmark Datasets

Evaluation was conducted using representative technical resumes across Machine Learning, Data Analytics, and Fullstack Engineering benchmarked against an authentic Machine Learning Engineer job description requiring PyTorch, Transformers, Deep Learning, SQL, and Docker.

Evaluation Benchmark Model Semantic Proximity Technical Skill Match Composite Compatibility Inference Latency (CPU)
Traditional Keyword Matching ATS 28.5% 75.0% 47.1% < 5 ms

B. Semantic Robustness under Synonym Substitution

To verify semantic invariance, technical terminology was systematically substituted with paraphrases (e.g., 'Deep Artificial Neural Networks' for 'Deep Learning' and 'Computational Linguistics' for 'NLP'). Under traditional keyword ATS, the score plummeted by 38.2%, whereas ResumeMind exhibited a variance of only 1.8%, demonstrating high semantic resilience.

VI. Conclusion and Future Work

This research presented ResumeMind, an end-to-end deep learning system overcoming the rigid vocabulary limitations and opacity of conventional Applicant Tracking Systems. By combining bidirectional BERT transformer embeddings, attention-weighted mean pooling, cosine distance in latent space, and boundary-enforced skill extraction, ResumeMind delivers objective evaluation alongside actionable skill gap diagnostics.

Future work will focus on integrating long-sequence transformer backbones (Longformer / BigBird), training domain-specific Named Entity Recognition (NER) models for zero-shot skill detection, and incorporating generative language models for personalized resume optimization.
